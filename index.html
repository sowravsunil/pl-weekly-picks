How it works

The numbers

Every match gets three chances that add up to 100%: home win, draw and away win. The biggest of the three is the pick.

Where they come from

Three separate models, each built on Premier League results going back to 2000:

  • a team rating that moves after every result
  • a goals model that estimates how many each side should score and concede, with recent matches counting most
  • a machine-learning model that looks at team strength, recent form and fixture congestion

The page shows the average of the three. None of them sees team news, injuries or betting prices.

Confidence

Strong
The pick is 65% or more and all three models agree.
Lean
55% or more, and all three agree.
Toss-up
Anything else.

Over the previous five seasons, Strong picks came off about three times in four and Leans about seven times in ten.

Keeping score

Two measures, both set against the bookmakers:

  • Favourite won: how often the most likely result actually happened.
  • Probability score: also rewards sensible chances for the draw and the outsider. Lower is better.

The bookmakers' figures are average closing prices with their margin taken out. Closing prices already reflect team news and late money, so getting close to them is a good result.

Live and backfilled

Live picks were published here before kick-off and can't change once a match starts. Live publishing began in gameweek 8. Earlier matches are backfilled, worked out afterwards using only results available before each kick-off, so the whole season can be judged the same way.

Draws

About one match in four ends level, but a draw is rarely the single most likely result. That's why the pick is nearly always a win for one side.
